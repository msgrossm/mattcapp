### 1. What I did
- I asked "write a one sentence summary of this New York Times opinion piece by
  David Herzberg, a drug historian at Buffalo, in an aggressive, offensive
  voice," and I didn't like the response.
- I wanted a short one liner rather than a whole paragraph, so I moved into
  examples to follow.

### 2. Artifact
"Trump's blowing up boats to save a heartland that's been high since the
Gilded Age — America didn't get poisoned by foreigners, it got a
prescription."

### 3. Claim
I think that a few shot prompt will reap the best response to the problem
"write a one sentence summary of this New York Times opinion piece by David
Herzberg, a drug historian at Buffalo, in an aggressive, offensive voice,"
because refining the prompt based on human examples will yield a more natural
result.

### 4. Question
Because AI is a reflection of human knowledge, skills, and experiences, does
giving examples to Claude to work with generally yield a higher quality work
product?

### 5. Reflection
- My standard: The prompt has to make me laugh or nod my head. I won't judge
  based off length, since I asked Claude midway for shorter answers.
- Based off this criteria, my favorite response was the second, few shot
  response. It made me laugh a little bit. The other responses did not yield a
  similarly satisfactory result.
- It seems to me that after mentioning some of my favorite comedians, Claude's
  work product improved.
